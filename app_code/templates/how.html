How it works

A clinical equation, plus where you live.

Your score starts from the Framingham general cardiovascular risk equation, a published and validated calculator doctors have used for years. Heartscape then applies an illustrative adjustment for your borough's environment.

01

You answer

Age, sex, blood pressure, cholesterol, smoking, diabetes and whether you take blood-pressure treatment, plus lifestyle questions and your borough.

02

Framingham base score

Seven clinical factors go into the published equation (D'Agostino et al., 2008), giving your chance of a first heart attack, stroke or other cardiovascular event in 10 years.

03

Borough adjustment

Your borough's multiplier (×0.85 to ×1.20) scales that risk up or down to reflect air pollution and green space. This step is illustrative, not clinically validated.

04

Result and guidance

You see your risk band, how many percentage points each factor adds, your borough's air quality, and personal next steps based on all of your answers.

What goes into the score

Only factors from the validated equation change the number. Everything else shapes your guidance.

Used for guidance only

Family history, BMI, physical activity, alcohol, stress and sleep all matter for heart health, but they are not in the Framingham equation, so they don't change your number. Family history in particular can mean your true risk is higher than shown.

To check the maths, Heartscape reproduces both worked examples published with the equation exactly: 10.48% for a 61-year-old female smoker and 15.62% for a 53-year-old man with diabetes and treated blood pressure.

The borough adjustment scales risk as a hazard: adjusted = 1 − (1 − risk)multiplier. For a 10% base risk, the cleanest borough gives about 8.6% and the most polluted about 11.9%.

Know the limits

US population

Framingham was developed in a mostly white US population and can overestimate risk for some UK groups. NHS practices use QRISK3, which also covers ethnicity and deprivation.

Existing heart disease

The equation is for people without cardiovascular disease. If you have had a heart attack or stroke, your risk is higher and this tool does not apply.

Illustrative environment

Borough multipliers reflect published links between pollution and heart disease, but their exact size is our estimate and hasn't been validated.

Not a diagnosis

This is an educational estimate. Only a clinician with your full history can assess your risk and advise on treatment.
